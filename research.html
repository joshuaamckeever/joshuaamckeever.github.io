Research

My research explores how gene regulation shapes cellular identity and immune tolerance. I am interested in the ways cells establish distinct gene expression programs and how those programs support healthy immune system development.

Vitamin D Receptor and immune tolerance

During my doctoral research at the University of Chicago, I studied how the Vitamin D Receptor (VDR) influences gene expression in medullary thymic epithelial cells (mTECs). These cells help the developing immune system learn to distinguish the body’s own tissues from potential threats.

My work examined how VDR contributes to transcriptional diversity and the presentation of self-antigens in the thymus, with the goal of understanding how changes in gene regulation may affect central immune tolerance.

Approaches

I use genomic and computational approaches to investigate gene regulation, including single-cell RNA sequencing, single-cell chromatin accessibility profiling, multiomic analysis, and transcription factor motif analysis.

Research interests

Gene regulation, immune system development, cellular identity, transcriptional heterogeneity, and the genomic mechanisms underlying immune tolerance.
